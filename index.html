01 Field-service platform Live

Travelling Technicians

A doorstep repair service that can be found, booked, tracked and followed up — without anyone chasing a phone call.

Someone cracks a screen in Burnaby at nine at night. They search, land on a page for their city and their exact device, see a price and a warranty before they commit, check that we actually cover their postal code, and book a technician to come to them in three steps. If they'd rather ask a human first, the chat is a real customer-service surface, not a decorative bubble.

Every booking lands in a real-time admin surface instead of an inbox. It routes to a technician, moves through repair status, generates a warranty record, takes payment through Stripe, sends transactional mail through Resend, and closes with a review request. The chat and voice assistants share the same tools that admin runs on — so the automated answer and the human answer come from one source of truth. Canadian privacy and consumer-compliance flows are part of it, not an afterthought.

02 Privacy-led data product Live

Immigration Timeline

A prediction product that is careful about what it doesn't know — and says so.

People waiting on an immigration decision are anxious, and anxious people are easy to sell false certainty to. I didn't want to build that. So the interface is narrow and quiet, it gives you a window instead of a date, and it says out loud that you shouldn't book a flight on an estimate. The restraint is the product.

  • Report your milestones
  • →
  • See a range
  • →
  • Read the methodology
  • →
  • Come back tomorrow

Reported dates are aggregated behind a minimum threshold before anything is published, so no single applicant can be reverse-engineered out of a reading. Access is passwordless — there's no account to breach. The model's training date is stated on the page rather than implied, and the honest answer to "how confident are you" is built into the data model instead of being written into a footer.

  • Aggregate privacy thresholds
  • Passwordless access
  • Published methodology
  • Range-based modelling

Keeping live systems honest

Launch is a checkpoint, not the finish.

Four things I check on my own sites, because live systems drift.

  • One source for every numberReview counts, locations and prices come from data — not a sentence someone forgot to update.
  • One canonical addressApex and www resolve to the same place, with canonical tags that agree.
  • Watch what breaks after deployA counter that renders as 0 is a data problem, and only real page checks catch it.
  • Listen to the floorWhoever uses the admin every day writes the best bug report you'll ever get.
